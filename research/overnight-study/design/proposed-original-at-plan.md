## What must be learned before authoring

The digital analysis and official preview study already distinguish phrase boundaries and articulation changes. They do not identify all instruments reliably, and no auditory listening or human playtest is claimed. The user accepts digital audio and official timing as the available musical evidence. Complete a full 173.8 s evidence annotation using observable attack, sustain, register and energy roles; instrument names remain provisional. Select candidate fresh attacks from official onset times corroborated by spectral flux, and candidate new voices only from audio evidence with phrase-level justification. A listening pass could refine these choices if later available; the accepted evidence method is official timing plus digital audio. Compare each proposed repeated phrase against its source/digital evidence before any later authoring.

## Playing-line architecture — updated user constraint

**One main PLAYING line at a time.** The planned ordinary arrangement keeps one horizontal judgment locus and a single ordinary approach axis. The two hand zones are lateral parts of that same locus, not independent playing rails. Four voices mean independent musical/contact roles, not four judgment axes. All table references to fans, rectangles, frames or guides describe decorative composition unless explicitly identified as technical carrier motion.

Technical carrier lines may own an individual note to shape its approach or subtle lateral displacement. Their head must resolve onto the current main playing line at judgment. Engine-line count is therefore not playing-line count. Empty decorative lines may open, fan or resolve in negative space while the active judgment locus remains obvious. Carrier and empty-line roles must be documented separately, and cosmetic changes must not disguise a new contact demand. During a hold the head/contact point stays on the main playing locus: carrier approach motion settles by head arrival and remains stable through the tail. Empty decorative lines may continue moving without changing that contact.

No box, upside-down or simultaneous opposed-axis gimmick is proposed for this original baseline. Such special passages would require their own explicit reviewed musical purpose and pre-cue, not an AT default. The first implementation, if later released, should establish the complete single-axis rhythm/lateral flow before adding carriers or empty-line spectacle. Study the source ending of Der Schneid for how long hold bars and empty-line choreography retain a coherent playing locus, not as permission to multiply input axes.

## Candidate timing sources and acceptance criteria

For each proposed phrase, use three separate evidence records: official IN note/hold timing as an articulation reference; decoded audio attacks/continuity/register energy as corroboration; and the new arrangement's musical/contact role. Source chart coordinates and wholesale note sequences are not a composition method. A shared attack belongs to the song, but choosing its new articulation, voice, lateral motif and hand role must be independently justified.

Candidate fresh attacks can begin with official timestamps supported by nearby positive spectral-flux peaks. The measured 35 ms neighborhood is a useful corroboration window at the current 11.61 ms hop size, not a correctness rule. A candidate new attack absent from IN needs stronger phrase-level evidence: a recurring digital attack in comparable phrases and a reason to expose an additional voice. If ambiguous, leave space. Do not populate every grid subdivision because the soundtrack is loud.

Candidate holds begin with official sustained articulation/duration and digital continuity in an appropriate register. A gap between attacks alone does not justify extending a hold. Candidate drags correspond to connected figures: the task is sustained travelling contact rather than fresh strikes. Candidate flicks require a credible energetic release and sufficient exit/recovery room. Instrument names may stay “low-register sustained voice,” “foreground articulated attacks,” or “connected high-register figure” until evidence supports a more specific label.

Before later implementation, each challenging motif needs its own short record: phrase bounds, source/digital timestamps, chosen evidence tier, contact roles, lateral contour, release windows and intended evolution at its next return. Independent review must be able to reject a motif without changing every section generated from one template. No automatic uniform densification is planned.

## Four-finger choreography contract

Use L-index / L-middle / R-index / R-middle as a planning vocabulary, not a rigid requirement for every player's hands. Initial broad hand zones keep each hand on its own half of the screen. A provisional four-station layout is native x = −360, −120, +120, +360 on the same playing line; stations are 17.78% of viewport width apart. At 200 mm viewport width that is about 35.6 mm, which is an ergonomic hypothesis for testing, not a settled device recommendation. Match note width and finger separation before committing to those coordinates. Avoid thumb-based assumptions because this AT is designed for tabletop or supported-device multifinger play; the final device-size recommendation awaits testing.

**Sustain plus answer:** a middle finger holds an outer sustained voice while its hand's index plays an inner answer. If both hands sustain, two indices remain available. Any inner two-note chord uses those two free indices; do not ask a held middle finger for another fresh attack. End holds early enough to provide a genuine release interval before their fingers enter a tap relay. A nominal same-timestamp tail/head swap is not accepted as a comfortable release.

**Drag thread plus answer:** one index tracks a connected series of drags. It remains occupied while travelling, even if individual drag heads are not simultaneous. The same hand's middle finger may add an isolated outer accent only with clear separation; the other hand carries fresh foreground taps. Release the thread before demanding that index tap or reverse into a flick. A drag path traversing a held target can cause unintended judgment or force repositioning and must be rejected in review.

**Four-finger relay:** assign four adjacent targets to the four fingers; roll their order around an audible arpeggiated or articulated figure. Several fresh notes separated by a quarter-beat at 225 BPM imply66.7 ms globally, but four-finger rotation gives266.7 ms per finger. That arithmetic is only a hypothesis: spacing, hand position and fatigue still require playtesting. Avoid same-finger rapid retriggers while its hand is also holding.

**Four-contact accent:** permissible only where four meaningful attacks or simultaneous voice roles are audible. A chord's spacing must allow two fingers per hand without covering the center. It should be introduced with prior simpler instances, not become an untelegraphed peak. Counts include holds already in progress and travelling drag contacts; a three-head chord during two holds is five, not three.

**Flick exits:** choose a consistent outward or phrase-following movement where possible. Its moving finger must have room before its next target. No forced inward/outward reversal on adjacent rapid attacks, and no flick exit directed through another finger's hold. The native flick type does not encode directional judgment; direction in this plan describes comfortable intended motion, not a game rule.

**Candidate release margins:** seek at least0.5 beat (133.3 ms) between a sustained-contact release and a distant new head by that finger; at least1 beat (266.7 ms) before a substantial direction-changing flick or cross-zone relocation. These are review thresholds, not validated ergonomic limits. Anything tighter needs explicit local justification and later playtest evidence.

## Visual progression and approach requirements

The gameplay arc keeps one main horizontal playing line. The cosmetic arc is empty shallow fan → four-voice rectangular framing → empty fan exposition → lateral-zone guides → familiar flat climax → final sustained pair with restrained empty-line spectacle. Line movement follows phrase changes. The major exposition occurs during 81–83.2 s and 104–112.5 s;121.1–124.3 s supplies another reset. There is no continuous global swivelling.

Initial readability hypothesis: ordinary demanding notes should have roughly0.8–1.2 s of useful onscreen approach; especially unfamiliar independent figures need nearer1.0 s. A line being visible is insufficient if the note's trajectory appears from an edge too late. Speed tuning must account for line height, rotation, note-side and aspect ratio together. Do not use a single nominal scroll speed as proof of read time.

Use source-native units correctly: line/note coordinates target1350×900 conceptual layout, but actual rendered height depends on aspect. Rotation is degrees. Native opacity is0–255, whereas official JSON opacity is0–1. The converter/native runtime multiply/divide255; a native opacity of1 is almost invisible. UI-level normalized values must be labelled and compiled to native units explicitly.

Before approving a later chart, evaluate each note's trajectory from at least 1.2 s before judgment, sample the renderer at 60 fps around risky sections, and inspect paths under every simultaneous hold body. Report earliest entry, longest visibility gap and final continuous read interval. Inspect 16:9 and a narrower tablet/phone aspect separately. Imported reference previews with a black background are acceptable for study but do not prove the eventual song illustration preserves contrast.

## Quality gates for a future implementation

- **Study release:** finish the digital/source phrase annotations, explain reference lessons with exact windows, and receive explicit release of the active plan gate. This is required before original note creation.
- **Music:** every added attack/hold/drag/flick has a named voice/gesture, with chosen timestamps checked against audio and phrase context. Repeated motives evolve where music changes. Preserve mapped rests unless a reviewed reason changes them.
- **Physical:** store intended hand/finger assignments for every challenging phrase. Check occupied holds, drag travel intervals, release margins, world-space target separation and consecutive same-finger displacement. Automatic maximum-contact checks cannot establish comfortable play.
- **Visual:** settle a new frame before its notes' approach begins; preserve at least one stable landmark. Inspect continuous approach, hold occlusion, flick exits and note-side changes. Record every unresolved frame/device limitation.
- **Originality:** no bulk coordinate replacement of IN or SP, no copied note-sequence blocks, no SP letter/tile tribute. A shared musical attack may be charted because it belongs to the music, but the new voice selection/choreography must be independently justified.
- **Technical:** deterministic native project; canonical rational beat triples; positive finite speeds; finite events; notes/holds inside audio; native deserialize/load evidence; playable export/roundtrip timing and opacity checks; source corpus preserved.
- **Review:** render and inspect full-song plus continuous high-risk excerpts. An expert multifinger playtest must judge comfort, timing, stamina and difficulty before calling the AT17 estimate credible. Automated validation and autoplay are reported separately.

## Remaining uncertainties

Instrument-level labels remain uncertain because digital/visual observations do not identify every voice. The user accepts that limitation; proposed choices must instead cite observable attack/sustain/register/energy evidence and remain conditional when that evidence is weak. World-space motion, actual contact occupancy, density, scroll speeds and read intervals cannot be measured for an uncreated chart; no implementation gate is claimed passed. Device ergonomics and level estimate remain untested. A catalog/wiki absence of AT is good current evidence, but the eligibility check should be refreshed immediately before later publication.
