## Rrhar'il — AT versus IN semantic comparison

Equal totals can conceal radically different articulation and sustained demand. This is supporting numerical evidence, not a claim that every Rrhar'il gesture has been physically reviewed in this document.

The useful principle is purposeful note semantics. ## Resulting standards for the proposed AT

1. Every new layer must have a named audible role and a phrase-specific reason.
2. Difficult sections alternate with enough recovery to make the section change legible.
3. Four-finger play must create independence and relay, not just simultaneous-count decoration.
4. Connected drags consume a travelling contact; sustained holds consume an occupied contact. Neither can be counted as interchangeable taps.
5. Geometry is introduced before it must be read under pressure. Stable anchors survive orientation changes.
6. Inspect approach trajectories, hold bodies and flick exits at actual target aspect ratios, not just a nice screenshot at impact.
7. A proposed AT17 label remains a hypothesis until experienced players test timing, readability, stamina and device ergonomics.

## Updated distinction: arrangement versus special gimmick

The user clarified the normal baseline: ONE main playing line at a time, regular notes sharing its approach axis. Multiple engine lines can carry individual notes to create approach/lateral motion; the carrier resolves onto the main playing locus at judgment. Empty lines can be pure spectacle. Engine-line count alone therefore says little about input-axis complexity.

The Bounded/Distorted fan and tile windows above are special geometric vocabulary to understand, not a default justification for the proposed AT to use multiple judgment rails. Study the squished rhythm/lateral arrangement first; distinguish fresh strikes, held occupancy and travelling drags from cosmetic line choreography. Boxes, inversion and simultaneous opposed axes are explicitly special gimmicks. Any such passage needs isolated musical purpose, teaching and recovery.
