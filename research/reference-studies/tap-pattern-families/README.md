## Main correction

Wide-movement trills are one movement style. They emphasize continuous alternating fresh hits and coherent substantial travel between successive hits of each hand. They are not the universal optimum for dense Tap charting.

The first requested group contains especially useful **three-hit groups and concentrated repeated-hit streams**. Cross Soul permits co-located alternating rolls in some passages. 70 Minutes Fighters and Re: End of a Dream show how companion contacts can instead make a whole group naturally belong to one hand. One cannot diagnose all three from Tap coordinates alone.

The second group emphasizes **simultaneous attacks, single/chord interleaving, rhythmic gaps, short bursts, and contact-dependent division of work** in the examined passages. The useful unit is often a small two-hand action pattern, not a long unbroken trill. These are passage-level descriptions: both groups contain other styles and their boundary is not absolute.

## What changes in future analysis

1. Classify the current passage's intended action first: wide-movement trill, co-located roll, a same-hand repeated-hit group, anchored layering, repeated chords, or contact-driven interlocking. These are working descriptions, not claims of universal community terminology.
2. Keep the timeline as simultaneous action sets with empty slots and active-contact intervals. Do not flatten chords, erase rests, or remove non-Tap occupancy when studying dense Taps.
3. Compare plausible fingerings against that complete timeline. Distinguish repeated position, repeated hand, and repeated two-hand chord. Only one of those is a single-hand repeat.
4. Record total Tap-object density, distinct strike-onset density, and each candidate hand's fresh-hit density separately. None alone measures difficulty.
5. Preserve the user's musical requirement: intentional same-hand double/triple strikes need concrete musical timing and phrasing. A source pattern's repeated subdivision is structural evidence, not permission to insert unrelated repeats or a substitute for hearing the intended music when authoring.
6. Keep contact feasibility and natural release/re-entry as general checks. Keep broad travel and nearly continuous alternation scoped to wide-movement trills. A different style can deliberately prioritize articulation, synchronization, hand independence or contact changes.
7. Apply labels to passages, not whole songs. A chart can switch styles, and the entry/exit between them is part of the arrangement.
